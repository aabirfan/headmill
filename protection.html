What we arrange

Cover for the things that matter

Every recommendation starts with your situation. We only recommend what fits, never a policy for the sake of it.

Life Insurance

The cornerstone of most protection plans: a lump sum for your family if you die during the term. Whether it's clearing the mortgage or replacing your contribution to the household, we size the cover to what your family would actually need, not a round number off a sales script.

Critical Illness Cover

A tax-free lump sum if you're diagnosed with a serious condition covered by the policy, such as heart attack, stroke or certain cancers. Insurers' definitions vary enormously, so we compare what's actually covered, not just the monthly premium.

Income Protection

Often the most overlooked cover of all: a monthly income if illness or injury stops you working, paid until you're back on your feet or the policy ends. We match deferred periods to your sick pay and savings, so you're not paying for cover you'd never use.

Family & Whole-of-Life

Longer-term planning for the people you'll leave behind, from family income benefit to whole-of-life policies that pay out whenever you die, not just within a set term. Useful for funeral costs and inheritance considerations, and we'll tell you honestly if it's not right for you.

Protection questions

Good to know

Do I really need life insurance?

Not everyone does. If nobody depends on your income and you have no mortgage, you may not need it at all, and we'll say so. But if a partner, children or a joint mortgage rely on you, cover is usually far cheaper than people expect, especially when arranged younger.

What's the difference between critical illness and income protection?

Critical illness pays a one-off lump sum if you're diagnosed with a specific condition listed in the policy. Income protection pays a monthly income for any illness or injury that stops you working, for as long as the policy allows. They solve different problems. Many people benefit from one, some from both, and we'll help you work out which.

Is insurance through a broker more expensive?

No. You'll typically pay the same as or less than going direct, because we compare across the whole market rather than one insurer's shelf. Just as importantly, we check the policy definitions and exclusions, not just the premium, so you're less likely to find gaps at claim time.

What is putting a policy in trust?

A trust is a simple legal arrangement that directs a policy payout to the people you choose. It can help the money reach them faster, usually without waiting for probate, and may keep it outside your estate for inheritance tax purposes. It isn't right for every situation, so we'll explain the pros and cons for yours.
